PPG 002
Détournement, Collage and Desire
November 2025  ·  Peckham Library
We traced a throughline from Dadaism, looking at the work of Max Ernst, Hans Arp and Hugo Ball, before reading Tristan Tzara's invigorating "How to Make a Dadaist Poem."
Folding in the Situationist International's concept of Détournement, "the re-use of pre-existing artistic elements in a new ensemble" — members had arrived having collected material in preparation: photos, religious leaflets, sales flyers, surreal adverts and scrawls found around South London.
We read Brion Gysin's Cut-Up Method and Gregory Corso. Then in the second half of the session, everyone was handed paper, scissors and glue. The atmosphere was frenetic.
After reading local artist Amy Giles' piece Chant IV, we passed the microphone around and read out what we'd made — poems cut together from detritus and the language of our urban environment.
Texts in the session
  • Tristan Tzara  — How to Make a Dadaist Poem
  • Guy Debord  — Détournement as Negation and Prelude
  • Brion Gysin  — The Cut-Up Method
  • Gregory Corso  — "Cut-up of Eisenhower Speech" and "Mine Own Poem"
  • Amy Giles  — Chant IV
